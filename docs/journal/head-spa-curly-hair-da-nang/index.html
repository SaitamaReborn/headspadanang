Curly hair · 8 min · 27 October 2026

The comb is the whole problem: curly and textured hair in a Da Nang basin

Whether a Vietnamese head spa works on curly, coily or very thick hair · which two of the ten steps were built for straight hair, what the breakage literature says about the wet detangle, the one tier textured hair genuinely profits from, and the five sentences that fix the rest.

In short
  • Count the steps and almost none of them are about your hair. On a long Da Nang menu the foot soak, the face work, the steam, the stones, the scalp scrub, the pressure points, the neck and shoulder massage and the tea are indifferent to curl pattern. Two steps are not: the wet detangle and the blow-dry.
  • Stop the comb, not the water. Combing studies in the Journal of Cosmetic Science describe fibres that snap on impact when a comb catches, with almost no stretch involved, while tensile work shows break stress dropping as humidity climbs. Da Nang runs at 75 to 85 percent. Your hair is therefore already at the weak end of its range before anyone lays a hand on it.
  • Our position reverses what we told people with cropped heads: of all body types, textured hair is the one that earns the long herbal tiers. The dwell time and the nourishing mask both act on fibre. You have plenty of it. Across five houses the 70-to-90-minute band costs 450K to 900K, and the pick prices its 80-minute Signature at 500K, mask included.
  • The styled finish is the only step in the hour that can send you home worse off than you arrived. A round brush and fifteen minutes of hot air on tension is a blow-out, and you will be washing it back out tonight. Sấy mát thôi, đừng dùng lược tròn nhé keeps the dry and loses the brush.

A trade built on the straightest hair there is

The question tends to arrive sideways, from the pavement, with a phone in hand and the menu already open: will they know what to do with my hair? Hardly anyone asks it about the massage. Those asking have curls, coils, waves or just a very great deal of hair. They have spent years figuring out which chairs back home are safe, and they are not going to hand the whole head to a stranger at a basin because of a photograph.

The hesitation is reasonable, and the audience it belongs to is growing fast enough that the houses here are meeting it more often. Vietnam News Agency reported in late September 2026 that Vietnam went into the twelfth World Spa Awards as one of eleven countries shortlisted for World's Best Wellness Destination, alongside Switzerland, Italy, India and Indonesia, with Switzerland taking the title. A trade that spent decades serving the neighbourhood is now selling the same ninety minutes to visitors whose hair has nothing in common with the hair it was designed around.

That difference has been measured. Franbourg, Hallegot, Baltenneck, Toutain and Leroy, writing in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology in 2003, put hair from Caucasian, Asian and African donors through cross-section imaging, tensile testing and water-swelling measurement, and found no difference in the internal structure of the fibres at all. What differed was geometry and mechanics. East Asian hair came out the thickest and the roundest in cross-section; African hair the most oval, with far more variation in shape. A later commentary in the cosmetic-science press recalculated their diameters into the conventional major-over-minor ratio and got 1.78 for African hair, 1.33 for Caucasian and 1.23 for Asian. A round fibre with no curvature in it is the easiest thing in the world to comb wet, and it is what every comb, every round brush and every piece of muscle memory on this street was built around.

Then the useful correction, because the fear is usually imported from a different appointment. A head spa is not a haircut. Nobody is making a decision about your shape, nobody is cutting curls dry or wet or at all, and the long roster of things a curl-illiterate stylist can get wrong mostly does not apply here. The walkthrough lays out what actually arrives, and once you have it in front of you the risk shrinks to two steps out of ten.

Eight steps that do not care, two that do

Work from a real menu rather than a general impression. Reborn Nails & Retreat, the house this guide picks, lists its 80-minute Signature as a herbal foot soak and tea, facial cleansing and exfoliation, a quartz-stone massage, herbal steam and a mask, scalp exfoliation, the double herbal wash, a nourishing hair mask, neck, shoulder and hand massage, the blow-dry, and last of all fruit and tea. Read it with curls in mind. Nearly all of it is good news.

Foot soak, face, steam and stones all take place nowhere near your hair. As for the scalp exfoliation and the pressure work (the bấm huyệt that forms the spine of the Vietnamese version), they happen on the skin beneath the hair rather than on the fibre. Most guests still remember the neck and shoulder segment a week later, and it has no opinion whatsoever on curl pattern. The tea at the end is tea.

Which leaves the wet detangle and the finish. Those two are where a technician's hands make decisions about your hair, and they are the only two worth preparing for. Everything below is about them.

One structural note that makes both easier to control: in a gội đầu dưỡng sinh room you are reclined with your neck in a basin cradle and your head below the level of your body, which means your hair is behind you and out of your sight for most of the hour. You will not see the comb coming. That is exactly why the sentences in this piece belong at the counter, before you lie down, rather than in the middle of the wash.

The comb is the risk. The water mostly is not

The breakage mechanism here is not the one most people worry about. Reviews of combing and brushing damage in the Journal of Cosmetic Science describe something closer to impact than to stretching: a comb hits a tangle and stops, and the fibre fails at effectively no extension. The same literature also finds measurably less breakage with surface treatments that make hair easier to comb. Lubrication, in other words, counts for more than how gentle the hand holding the comb is.

Humidity stacks on top of that, and this is where Da Nang stops being a general case. On textured hair, tensile studies report break stress falling as humidity rises, because water interferes with the hydrogen bonds that hold the cortical matrix together in a dry fibre. Da Nang's monthly humidity series stays between roughly 75 and 85 percent across the year, peaking in the wet months. Your hair therefore reaches the basin already at the soft end of its mechanical range, before anyone has run water over it.

For tightly curled hair the numbers are less forgiving again. Fracture work in the same literature found African hair breaking at lower strains and showing every type of fracture pattern, where Caucasian hair mostly produced the clean smooth break. That particular comparison rested on a single donor, which is worth knowing before anyone builds a rule on it, and the broader cosmetic-science argument is that grooming force rather than any weakness inside the cortex is what accounts for the difference. Both readings point at the same object on the trolley.

Now the thing people do worry about, which deserves to be set down. Hygral fatigue, the idea that repeated swelling and shrinking of the fibre damages it, is genuinely contested. A Journal of Cosmetic Science paper on coconut oil reported reduced swelling and tied hygral fatigue to cuticular and cortical damage, though its subject was the oil rather than the cycling. Michelle Wong, a cosmetic chemist who writes publicly as Lab Muffin, calls it a persistent myth and argues the imaging usually offered in support has a simpler explanation. Nobody has run a controlled trial on repeated wet-and-dry cycles in curly hair. Our reading: the double wash that Da Nang menus print is two wet cycles and that is fine, and the five seconds in which a fine comb is pulled through the back of your head is not.

So the ask is specific, and one sentence covers it. Đừng chải lược, dùng tay gỡ giúp mình nhé asks them to leave the comb and separate it with their fingers, which the technician's hands are already doing in the lather. If they will use a comb anyway, cho mình lược thưa nhé asks for the wide-tooth one. Say either at the counter. By the time you get there, it will be on the trolley.

The finish is the step that can send you home worse off

Vietnamese finishing is a round brush and ten to fifteen minutes of hot air, and on straight hair it is not a flourish, it is the thing you paid for. On curly hair it is a blow-out performed without being asked for. Tension plus heat plus a brush is exactly how a curl pattern is removed, and the cruel part is the arithmetic afterwards: you leave with hair you did not order, you wash it out at your hotel that night, and the money you spent on the last quarter of the ritual bought you an extra wash.

Here I have to take issue with something this guide argues elsewhere. The wet-season piece says that in November the blow-dry stops being a courtesy and becomes the specification. For straight hair that is correct: at ninety percent humidity there is barely an evaporation gradient, and damp hair stays damp for hours. Thick curls are the exception that does not get to ignore it. In this city, air-drying a dense head is a two-hour project, and leaving a spa wet in October is miserable. Refusing the drying is not the answer. Refuse the brush.

The whole request is Sấy mát thôi, đừng dùng lược tròn nhé: cool air only, no round brush. Hands in place of a brush, with the dryer kept moving, get you most of the way dry with the pattern intact. If the house has a diffuser, it will bring it out when you mime the shape; without one, fingers and cool air beat a brush and heat on any day of the year. Have one more line ready for when you sit up, đừng chải nữa nhé (no more brushing), because a final tidy-up with a comb at the mirror is a reflex in every salon on earth.

The short version, if you remember nothing else from this piece: the only thing you are protecting is the last fifteen minutes. The first sixty-five were never a threat.

The tier textured hair actually profits from

This is where the recommendation inverts, and I should be plain that it contradicts what we published two days ago. The short-hair piece argues that under about three centimetres of hair the long rituals are the weakest line on a Da Nang menu, because the conditioning half of a herbal decoction and the hair mask both act on fibre, and a crop has barely any. Turn that argument around and it points the other way just as hard. A dense curly head is the body type with the most fibre in the room. That guest is the one on the books who collects the full value of the steep.

So for textured hair, the 70-to-90-minute band is the upgrade worth buying, and this guide does not give that sentence away lightly. The band runs 450K to 900K across the five houses with public menus; at the pick, an 80-minute Signature costs 500K with the nourishing mask written into it. The price table has the rest. What you buy is dwell time on a fibre with a lot of surface area and a mask with something to work on, plus the steam opening the hour instead of a rushed wash closing it.

Skip the short wash. The 25-to-30-minute tier costs 120K to 350K across four houses, superb value on a bob. On thick curly hair, though, the fixed cost of wetting, lathering and handling the length eats most of the booking before any massage begins. The session-length piece makes the general version of this case. With dense hair it is sharper, since the overhead is higher while the slot stays the same.

Watch what the herbs do and do not do while they sit there. A bồ kết and pomelo-peel decoction is a saponin cleanser: it lathers weakly by design and leaves far less slip on the fibre than the conditioner you use at home. Good for a scalp, awkward for detangling. That is the real reason the comb question matters more here than it would in a Western salon chair. On oily scalps, Da Nang blends reach for green tea, trà xanh; the herb breakdown covers what else goes into the pot and why the hour is the floor for any of it.

What to bring, and where to book it

Pack your own conditioner or leave-in. This is the one piece of luggage advice in the guide that is not optional, and it is no criticism of the houses: the local shelf is formulated for the roundest, straightest, least porous fibre in the literature, and you will not reliably find a curl cream on Nguyễn Văn Thoại at 19:00 on a Tuesday. Pass it over at the counter with mình dùng dầu xả của mình nhé, my own conditioner please, and it goes on where theirs would have gone. Nobody minds. The decoction, the part you came for, is unaffected.

In choosing a house, reset your expectations about the competence you need. TripAdvisor reviews of hair salons in Hanoi, Hội An and Ho Chi Minh City keep describing a stylist who understands curls as a rare find, which tells you where the baseline sits, and those reviews are about cutting. You are not buying a cut. What you need is compliance with four sentences, and the predictor of that is whether the floor understands you, not whether anyone has seen hair like yours before. At the pick, the owner Fiona runs the floor and speaks English, and the menu sits on an iPad in twenty languages: an advantage that sounds dull right up to the moment you are horizontal and someone is reaching for a brush.

Use the neighbourhood guide to narrow it down faster: the beach-side cluster in Mỹ An and An Thượng runs the English-language menus, while Hải Châu around Hàn Market serves a more local clientele at lower prices, with more pointing involved. Pick by walking distance. Then say your sentences.

A final branch. Should the Korean-style version with the scalp camera appeal, know that dense curly hair is its hardest case: the probe needs a clean parting against skin, and on a tight curl pattern getting one takes patience. A cropped head is the easy case and yours is the slow one. The comparison page sets out what each visit is really for. Otherwise, book the long herbal tier and hand over the conditioner, then say the comb sentence before you lie down rather than after you hear it.

Frequently asked

Can you get a head spa with curly hair?

Yes, and most of the ritual is indifferent to curl. On a long Da Nang menu, the foot soak, facial work, herbal steam, stone massage, scalp exfoliation, pressure points and the neck and shoulder segment all happen on skin or nowhere near your hair. Just two steps are built around straight hair: the wet detangle and the round-brush blow-dry. Ask for fingers rather than a comb and for cool air without the brush, and the remaining hour works exactly as advertised.

Will a Vietnamese head spa ruin my curls?

The wash will not; the finish can. Vietnamese finishing means a round brush plus ten to fifteen minutes of hot air, a blow-out whether you ordered one or not, and it removes a curl pattern the way heat and tension always do. Sấy mát thôi, đừng dùng lược tròn nhé asks for cool air and no round brush; đừng chải nữa nhé stops the tidy-up comb at the mirror. Given Da Nang's 75 to 85 percent humidity, keep the drying and drop the brush rather than leaving damp.

Do Vietnamese salons know how to handle curly hair?

On cutting, reviewers on TripAdvisor treat a curl-literate stylist in Vietnam as a lucky find, so keep expectations low there. A head spa is another kind of purchase: no one is cutting, shaping or styling to a plan, so you need a floor that understands four requests rather than curl expertise. English on the floor is what to select for, and that is why the beach-side houses in Mỹ An and An Thượng suit a first visit better than the cheaper rooms around Hàn Market.

Which head spa should I book if I have thick or long hair?

The 70-to-90-minute herbal tier, which runs 450K to 900K across the five Da Nang houses that publish menus; the guide's pick charges 500K for 80 minutes, nourishing hair mask included. Of all body types, dense hair is the one that collects the full value of a long herbal steep, because the conditioning half of a bồ kết decoction and the mask both act on fibre. Avoid the 25-to-30-minute wash at 120K to 350K: the fixed cost of wetting and handling a lot of hair eats most of that slot before the massage begins.
