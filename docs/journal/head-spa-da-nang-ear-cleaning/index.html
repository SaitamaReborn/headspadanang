The ear cleaning on the menu: what lấy ráy tai is, and when to turn it down

What the ten minutes of ear work bundled into Da Nang combos actually contains, why it makes you cough, what the ENT guideline says about instruments in the canal, and the four words that decline it without awkwardness.

In short
  • It is not part of the head spa ladder. Nothing between the 120K wash and the 850K luxury sequence on the tier table includes ear work · it arrives bundled inside barbershop and combo packages, which is why it surprises people.
  • The shiver and the cough are anatomy, not magic. The ear canal carries a branch of the vagus nerve, and that is why ten minutes of it lands out of all proportion to what is happening.
  • The specialist position is unambiguous on one item and cautious on the rest: ear candling is recommended against outright, and instruments in the canal are a risk you are choosing to take.
  • Decline at the counter, not on the lounger · Không lấy ráy tai nhé. Accepting it the day you fly, or the day you swim at Mỹ Khê, is the version worth avoiding.

What the ten minutes actually contains

The first thing to understand is that ear work is not an extension of the hair wash. The wash is warm, wet and blind: herbs steeping in a pot, bồ kết and pomelo peel and lemongrass, two shampoos and a pair of hands you never see. Ear cleaning is the opposite in every respect. The lights come up or a headlamp goes on, the technician's face moves to within a hand's width of yours, and a small tray appears.

On that tray, in most Da Nang houses: a thin steel loop on a handle · thìa ngoáy tai, literally an ear spoon · fine tweezers for anything the loop cannot lift, cotton buds, and a twirl of soft feather barbs on a stem. The loop does the scraping, the tweezers do the extraction, the feather does the long slow sweep at the end that most people remember afterwards. Some houses add a vibration step, laying a small steel fork or buzzing rod against the handle of the pick so the tremor travels down the shaft into the canal. Some warm a drop of oil in first. The whole sequence runs eight to fifteen minutes an ear-pair, which is why a package that quietly includes it is a package where a tenth of your booked time is not happening on your scalp.

Keep one distinction clear before you agree to anything, because the words blur on translated menus. Manual ear cleaning with instruments is one service. Ear candling · a hollow cone lit at one end and held in the ear, sold as xông tai and occasionally described in English as a warming or detox step · is an entirely different one. The 2026 site copy on a fair number of spas uses the phrases interchangeably. They are not interchangeable, and only one of them has a settled verdict.

Why ten minutes feels like an hour

There is a real reason the sensation is so disproportionate, and it is worth knowing because it also explains the odd things that happen to people mid-procedure. The external ear canal is one of the few places on the body where the vagus nerve comes to the surface, via a branch usually called Arnold's nerve. Mechanical stimulation there can set off a cough in a meaningful minority of people · the ear-cough reflex, a recognised clinical curiosity · and a good deal more often it produces a swallow, a shiver down one side, or the specific heavy drowsiness that head spa regulars in Vietnam will tell you is the point of the whole thing.

So the pleasure is not imagined. Neither, though, is the structural problem underneath it, which is that you have no way to supervise what is happening. In every other part of a ritual you can calibrate · mạnh hơn ạ for more pressure, nhẹ hơn ạ for less · and the technician adjusts on the next pass. With a steel loop inside a canal you cannot see, by the time a sensation registers as wrong the movement that caused it has already finished. That asymmetry is the honest argument against the service, and it is not an argument about Vietnamese hygiene standards. It would be just as true in a clinic.

What the guideline says, and the coastal footnote

The American Academy of Otolaryngology's clinical practice guideline on cerumen impaction is the reference most English-language advice traces back to, and its position is duller than either side of the internet argument. Earwax is not dirt. The canal clears itself by migrating outward, the wax is mildly acidic and carries antimicrobial proteins, and for most people the correct amount of intervention is none. The guideline advises against putting cotton swabs and similar objects into the canal, notes that cleaning attempts frequently push wax deeper rather than removing it, and flags that over-cleaning can irritate the canal and make infection and impaction more likely rather than less.

On candling it is not cautious at all: no evidence that it removes cerumen, documented burns and canal obstruction from dripped wax, and a direct recommendation against it. If a Da Nang menu offers you a candle, that is a firm no at any price, and the fact that the house sells it tells you something about the rest of the menu.

The same guideline lists the people who should not be improvising with their ears at all: anyone on anticoagulants, anyone diabetic or immunocompromised, anyone with a perforated eardrum or grommets, anyone with prior radiation to the head and neck, anyone with a narrow or bony canal. If you are in one of those groups, a salon is simply the wrong building.

Then there is the part specific to this city, which no general guideline is going to mention. Da Nang sits between 75 and 85 percent humidity most of the year, and people come here to get in the water · Mỹ Khê is a ten-minute walk from most of the An Thượng spa cluster. A canal that has just been stripped of its wax film and lightly abraded by a steel loop, then filled with warm seawater on the same afternoon, is a reasonably efficient recipe for otitis externa. If you have the ear work done, the sea is finished for that day. And if your ears are still blocked from the descent into Đà Nẵng airport, the answer is no on the grounds of timing alone · a canal that has not equalised is not a canal to go poking in.

Where it actually shows up on a Da Nang menu

Here is the practical shape of it. Ear cleaning has no line of its own anywhere on the ritual ladder this guide tracks · not at 120K for the 25-minute herbal wash, not at 380K for the hour, not at 500K for the 80-minute signature. It lives somewhere else entirely: inside combo and barbershop packages, the 90-minute all-in sequences that stack a shave, a facial, nail work, a body massage and a hair wash into one price. In those, ear work is an assumed component rather than a choice you were offered, and English-language reviews of the city's houses bear that out · people describe it turning up inside a package they booked for something else.

Reading across the review texts for the head spa and combo houses we track, the mentions of ear work skew clearly positive · the phrase people reach for is some version of clean, and more than one says it was the part of the package they did not expect to enjoy. There is also, in that same body of text, a visitor who paid for a full package and singled out the ear cleaning as the one element to skip, describing it as painful during and afterwards. One flagged complaint against a majority of happy ones is roughly what you would expect from a procedure whose outcome depends on a stranger's hand pressure inside a part of you that cannot be watched. It is not a scandal. It is a coin toss with a small downside and a small upside, and you are the one who decides whether to flip it.

One thing that is not ear cleaning, and gets mistaken for it constantly: the rinse. Plenty of standard rituals send a little warm water and shampoo around the outer ear during the double wash, and finish with a dry cloth pass. No tools, no headlamp, nothing entering the canal. That is part of the wash and there is no reason to decline it · the walkthrough covers where it falls in the sequence.

How to decline it, and how to accept it properly

Say it at the counter, while you are choosing the tier and before your shoes are off. Mid-ritual is the wrong moment for a negotiation you cannot see, and it is also the moment when saying no feels rude, which is exactly the pressure you want to avoid. Không lấy ráy tai nhé · no ear cleaning, please · does the entire job in four words and is heard a hundred times a week. Nobody is offended, nothing is subtracted from the massage, and in a combo the minutes usually go back into the scalp and shoulders, which is the better trade anyway.

If you are going to accept it, accept it properly. Ask what comes out of the pouch before it goes in: Dụng cụ có tiệt trùng không ạ? · are the instruments sterilised? · is a normal question here, not an insult, and watching what happens after you ask it is more informative than the answer. Take it in the morning rather than at the end of a ten-hour shift, say nhẹ tay nhé before they start, and treat the first genuinely sharp sensation as the end of the service rather than something to be polite about. No swimming, no dive, no flight that day.

My own position, having read every menu in this city: on a first visit, decline it. Not because it is dangerous · for most healthy people it is a small risk pleasantly packaged · but because it competes for the thing you came for. You have booked 60 or 80 minutes of somebody's full attention on a scalp that has been under a helmet and a humid sky all week, and giving a sixth of it to a steel loop and a feather is a bad allocation of an hour that costs 380K. Come back for the ears on the second visit, once you know the house and the house knows your head. If you have not chosen one yet, the neighbourhood guide is the place to start, and the combo-heavy barbershop houses are easy to tell apart from the ritual houses from the menu alone.

Frequently asked

Is Vietnamese ear cleaning safe?

For a healthy adult it is usually uneventful, but it is not a medical procedure and the risks are real: a scraped canal, wax pushed deeper, and in rare cases a perforated eardrum. Specialist guidance is that the canal cleans itself and that objects should not be inserted into it. Skip it entirely if you take anticoagulants, are diabetic or immunocompromised, have grommets or a perforated eardrum, or have just flown and your ears have not equalised.

Does a head spa in Da Nang include ear cleaning?

Usually not. Ear work has no line of its own on the standard ritual ladder, from the 120K 25-minute herbal wash up to the 850K luxury sequence. Where it turns up is inside combo and barbershop packages · the 90-minute all-in sequences with a shave, facial, nails and massage · where it is often assumed rather than offered. Many rituals do rinse the outer ear during the shampoo, which is a different thing and involves no tools.

How do I say no to ear cleaning in Vietnam?

Say it at the counter when you pick your ritual, not once you are reclined. Không lấy ráy tai nhé · no ear cleaning, please · settles it in four words with no awkwardness. Staff hear it constantly, nothing else is removed from the treatment, and in a package the time usually goes back into the scalp and shoulder work.

Why do I cough when someone cleans my ear?

Because a branch of the vagus nerve, known as Arnold's nerve, surfaces in the ear canal. Mechanical stimulation there triggers a cough in a minority of people and a shiver, a swallow or sudden heavy drowsiness in many more. It is a recognised reflex, not a sign that something has gone wrong.
