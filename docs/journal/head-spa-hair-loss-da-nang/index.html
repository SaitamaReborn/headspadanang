Hair in the basin: what a Da Nang head spa does, and does not do, for hair loss

Whether a Vietnamese head spa causes shedding or grows hair · what the towel is actually showing you, the arithmetic behind the one scalp-massage study every spa cites, the ginger problem in the pot, and what to book if thinning is the reason you came.

In short
  • The hair on the towel was already leaving. A healthy scalp sheds 50 to 100 hairs a day, most of them when the hair is handled, and shedding caused by a stressor arrives two to three months late. A basin collects; it does not pull.
  • The study every head spa quotes · nine men, four minutes a day, twenty-four weeks · found hair 7 microns thicker and not one extra hair. Matching that dose in Da Nang is roughly 25 visits to the 60-minute tier, about 9.5 million đồng.
  • If gừng · ginger · is in the blend and thinning is why you booked, ask for the pomelo-and-locust-pod pot instead. The one lab study on ginger's active compound found it slows hair growth, not the reverse.
  • Book the hour for the hour. The 60-minute ritual at 380K on the tier table is the right purchase for a scalp that has gone oily or itchy; a dermatologist is the right purchase for hair that is leaving in patches.

The line on every menu

Somewhere on the benefit list of almost any gội đầu dưỡng sinh page in Vietnam · the spa's own site, the Facebook post, the laminated card by the till · sit three phrases. Lưu thông máu, blood circulation. Giảm rụng tóc, less hair fall. Kích thích mọc tóc, stimulates hair growth. They appear in that order so often that they read as a single idea, and for a visitor who has been quietly watching a parting widen, they are the reason the 60-minute ritual gets booked over the 25-minute wash.

The three claims are not equally true, and they are worth taking one at a time, because the honest version is more useful than either the menu or the sceptic's shrug. One of them holds for exactly the length of the ritual. One has a single small study behind it and a great deal of arithmetic against it. One is, on the only evidence anyone has gathered, sometimes pointing the wrong way.

The question underneath all three is simpler and comes in two forms. Will the ritual make my hair fall out · because forty minutes of somebody's fingers in it feels as though it should · and will it make more grow. No, and not in any dose you would pay for. The rest of this piece is why.

What the towel is actually showing you

The moment that sends people to a search bar happens at about minute fifty. The technician lifts your head out of the cradle, presses the towel through your hair, and there on the white cotton is a dark tangle you did not expect. In a shower at home it goes down the drain unseen. Here the basin is a closed bowl, the water runs off clear over the herb sediment, and every hair that leaves you is collected and presented.

The numbers are dull and reassuring. The American Academy of Dermatology puts normal daily shedding at 50 to 100 hairs, and they do not fall evenly through the day · they come away when the hair is handled, which mostly means when it is washed. Skip washing for three days, as most visitors do in Da Nang's humidity because damp hair is hair you stop touching, and the next wash releases three days' worth at once. Two shampoos worked at massage pace, a comb through wet hair, a round brush under hot air: the ritual is the most thorough handling your head gets all week, so it is where the week's shedding shows up. It did not produce it.

The other thing to know is the lag. A hair that is going to fall because of something that happened to you · a fever, a crash diet, surgery, a bad three months at work · is pushed into its resting phase at the time of the stressor and lets go two to three months later. Dermatologists call it telogen effluvium and the delay is the diagnostic clue. Whatever comes away in a Da Nang basin in September was decided in June. Massage does not extract a hair that is still anchored; try it, gently, on your own crown. What the hands do is hurry along the ones that had already let go.

Two Da Nang-specific things get mistaken for loss and are neither. Helmet hair: every Grab bike ride puts a shared, sweat-lined helmet on your head, and the flat, itchy, thin-looking result at the end of the day is compression and salt. And the parting: at 16 degrees north the midday sun at Mỹ Khê burns the strip of scalp your hair does not cover, and a sunburnt, peeling parting looks like a widening one for about a week. If your scalp is burnt, say so at the counter · da đầu mình bị cháy nắng · and ask for warm water rather than hot, for the obvious reason.

The study every head spa quotes

There is one piece of research behind the growth claim, and it is better known than it is read. In 2016 a Japanese group published, in the journal Eplasty, a trial in which nine healthy men had a standardised scalp massage for four minutes a day, delivered by a device, for twenty-four weeks. At the end their hair was measurably thicker: mean shaft diameter rose from about 0.085 mm to 0.092 mm. Seven microns. The number of hairs did not change. In the same paper, human dermal papilla cells stretched in a dish for three days shifted their gene expression toward growth, which is the mechanism the authors proposed · mechanical stretching of the tissue under the follicle, not the blood circulation the menus talk about.

It is a real result and it is a small one, and it comes with a dose attached. Four minutes a day for twenty-four weeks is 672 minutes · a little over eleven hours of scalp work, spread thin, on the same head, every day. Now look at what a Da Nang ritual delivers. The 60-minute tier at around 380K puts hands on the scalp itself for something like twenty-five minutes: the two shampoos, worked in slowly, and a dry massage between them. The rest of the hour goes to neck, shoulders, face and the dry. To accumulate the study's eleven hours you would need roughly twenty-five visits · call it 9.5 million đồng · and the study's subjects did not take a week off between sessions. Our position is blunt: nobody should buy a head spa as a course of treatment for thinning hair. The arithmetic does not survive contact with a calculator, and the four minutes a day the research actually describes are free, done with your own fingers, in your hotel bathroom.

The other citation that circulates is a 2019 survey in Dermatology and Therapy of people with pattern hair loss who had tried a much heavier protocol · twenty minutes, twice a day, presses, pinches and stretches · recruited through a hair-loss website. Of 327 who attempted it, 68.9 percent said their loss had stabilised or partly reversed. A retrospective self-report with no control group, from a population that had signed up because they wanted it to work. It is encouraging and it is not evidence of the kind a dermatologist would act on. Neither paper says anything about a single visit. Neither says anything about herbs.

What the hands are doing, and which pass matters

None of this is an argument that the massage does nothing. If mechanical stretch is the mechanism, then the part of a Da Nang ritual that matters for the follicle is the part most first-timers barely register: the dry pass, when the technician's fingertips press and shift the scalp over the skull rather than sliding through lather. The Vietnamese tradition frames it as bấm huyệt, pressure on named points · thái dương at the temples, bách hội at the crown, phong trì where the neck meets the skull · and the technique is closer to lifting and moving the skin than to rubbing it. That is the pass to ask for more of. Mạnh hơn ở đỉnh đầu nhé · firmer on the crown · gets you the version the study was measuring.

The other scalp-facing step, the tẩy da đầu scrub bundled into tiers from about 380K upward, is doing a different job. It lifts sebum, sweat and product off the skin, and in a city where humidity sits between 75 and 85 percent that is a legitimate service for anyone whose scalp has turned oily and itchy by day four · the wet-season piece covers why that happens. It does nothing for a follicle. Menus that describe it as detoxifying the roots are describing a sensation, not a mechanism.

For what happens in which order, minute by minute, the walkthrough is the reference. The short version for this article: the shampoos are where the pleasure is, the dry pass is where the plausibility is, and the steam is for people who like steam.

Pomelo, ginger, and the pot

Then there are the herbs, which is what most people assumed the hair-growth line was about. Vỏ bưởi, pomelo peel, is the Vietnamese folk answer to hair fall · the country sells it as a leave-in spray, tinh dầu bưởi, from the shelves of every Long Châu and Pharmacity in the city, and the tradition is old enough to predate anyone selling anything. There is no human trial of pomelo peel on hair growth. There is also nothing in it that should worry you, and if you want the pot to lean that way, gội bưởi at the counter gets you the pomelo-heavy blend. What is actually in the bowl covers the rest of the pot.

Ginger is the one to watch. Gừng turns up in a fair number of Da Nang blends because it is warming and it smells the way a herbal wash is supposed to smell, and across East Asia it carries a folk reputation for stopping hair loss · there are ginger anti-hair-fall shampoos on every supermarket shelf in Vietnam. In 2013 a group in Guangzhou tested the claim in the only way it has been tested and published the result in PLoS One: 6-gingerol, ginger's main active compound, suppressed hair shaft growth in cultured human follicles and, applied to mice, lengthened the resting phase of the hair cycle. One study, in a dish and on mice, at concentrations nobody has measured in a Da Nang decoction. But it is the study, and it points the wrong way. If thinning is why you booked, không cho gừng nhé · no ginger, please · costs nothing and removes the one ingredient with evidence against it.

Bồ kết, the locust pod, is a different case and the herbs article makes it: saponin is a mild cleanser, the folk claim is shine and less shedding, and nobody has run the trial. It is the base of the pot for good reasons. Growing hair is not one of them.

Where hair loss is actually treated, and what to book instead

Some patterns are not a spa question at all. Hair coming away in coin-sized patches, a change you can see over weeks rather than months, a scalp that is red or painful, loss that began with a new medication: those belong to a dermatologist, and Da Nang has a public dermatology hospital, Bệnh viện Da liễu, as well as private clinics that see foreign patients. The treatments with trial evidence for pattern loss · topical minoxidil, and for men finasteride · are a pharmacy-and-doctor conversation. If you want to see your own follicles on a screen before deciding anything, the Korean-style houses in the city run scalp cameras, and the comparison page explains what that visit is and is not.

For everyone else · which is most people reading this · book the head spa for the reason it is good. The 60-minute tier at around 380K is the honest floor: two herbal shampoos, a dry pass on the crown you can ask to be firmer, the scrub if your scalp has gone oily, neck and shoulders, a proper blow-dry. Say tóc mình rụng nhiều · my hair is shedding a lot · and the technician will nod, hand you the towel to look at, and tell you what every technician in this city tells every visitor who says it: that this is normal. Then take the four minutes a day home with you. That part is free, and it is the only part the research was ever about.

Frequently asked

Does a head spa cause hair loss?

No. A healthy scalp sheds 50 to 100 hairs a day and most of them come away when the hair is handled, so the two shampoos, the comb and the blow-dry of a ritual release several days of normal shedding into one visible tangle on the towel. Massage cannot pull out a hair that is still anchored, and shedding driven by a stressor · illness, a crash diet, a hard stretch at work · appears two to three months later regardless of what touches your head.

Does a head spa help hair growth?

Not in any dose a visitor would buy. The one controlled study, nine men massaged four minutes a day for 24 weeks, found hair shafts about 7 microns thicker and no increase in hair count. That is roughly eleven hours of scalp work; a 60-minute Da Nang ritual delivers about 25 minutes of it, so matching the dose means around 25 visits at 380K. The daily home version with your own fingers is free.

Is Vietnamese herbal shampoo good for hair loss?

Pomelo peel (vỏ bưởi) is the traditional anti-hair-fall ingredient and has no human trial for or against it. Locust pod (bồ kết) is a mild saponin cleanser with a folk claim of shine and less shedding. Ginger (gừng), common in blends, is the one with evidence pointing the wrong way: a 2013 lab study found its main compound slowed hair growth in cultured follicles. Ask for a ginger-free blend if thinning is the reason you booked.

Why is my hair falling out in Vietnam?

Usually it is not falling out more · it is being washed less often. In 75 to 85 percent humidity people skip washes, so each one releases several days of normal shedding at once, and a head spa basin collects all of it where you can see it. Helmet compression and a sunburnt parting also mimic thinning. Real loss · patches, a visible change over weeks, a red or painful scalp · is a dermatologist appointment, and Da Nang has a public dermatology hospital.
