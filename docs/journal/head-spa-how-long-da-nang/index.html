Thirty, sixty, ninety: how long to book, and why the short wash is the expensive one

How long a Da Nang head spa should be · what a minute costs across six published menus, the one trial that cut the hour in half and lost the effect, why the extra quarter-hour is the cheapest on the menu, and the number to say at the counter.

In short
  • Divide price by stated minutes and the short wash loses. Across the six Da Nang houses that publish a full menu, a minute of head spa costs between 4.8K and 12.2K · and in five of those six, the longest ritual's minute is cheaper than the shortest's. Overhead is the reason: the bed, the towels, the dry and the tea cost the same whether you bought 25 minutes or 95.
  • The only trial that varied the dose found the half-hour did nothing. Sherman and colleagues, in the Annals of Family Medicine in 2014, randomised 228 adults with chronic neck pain to 30- or 60-minute massages at various weekly frequencies. The 60-minute arms cleared the bar; no 30-minute arm beat the wait list.
  • Going from 45 to 60 minutes is the cheapest quarter-hour on any of these menus · 90K to 130K for the extension, against ritual averages twice that per minute. By 45 minutes price has stopped being the variable that should decide anything.
  • Book 60 as your default: the price table puts that tier at 380K to 650K across six houses. Buy the 25-minute wash as a shower after the beach, not as a treatment, and treat the 80-plus tiers as a different product rather than a bigger one.

A minute of head spa costs between 4.8K and 12.2K

The menus in this city are priced per ritual with the minutes printed beside them, which makes an unglamorous sum available to anyone: divide one by the other. Do it across the six houses with a public price list · all of them sourced on the prices page · and the spread is wide. Herbal Spa's 45-minute wash at 550K works out at about 12.2K a minute. Len Spa's 30 minutes at 200K comes to 6.7K. Tigon Massage asks 350K for half an hour, which is 11.7K a minute, and 500K for the full hour, which is 8.3K.

Now read those pairs in the right direction. In five of the six houses, the longest ritual on the menu costs less per minute than the shortest. Herbal Spa falls from 12.2K at 45 minutes to 10K at 90. Panda Spa goes from 9.2K at an hour to 7.8K at 90 minutes. YURURI drops from 11.7K to 9.4K across the same stretch. Len Spa is almost flat, 6.7K down to 6.3K, which is its own kind of answer. The exception is the guide's pick: Reborn Nails & Retreat sells a 25-minute basic herbal wash at 120K, which is 4.8K a minute and the cheapest minute on any of the six menus, and its 80-minute signature at 500K works out at 6.3K · so there, uniquely, the minute gets dearer as the ritual gets longer.

The mechanism is not generosity. Almost everything that is not massage in a head spa is front-loaded and fixed: the bed turned over and wiped, towels laundered, herbs already simmering in the pot, a blow-dry, tea, two minutes at the counter with a card machine or a VietQR code. That bundle costs the house roughly the same whether you bought 25 minutes or 95. A short booking pays for all of it at full rate, which is why the cheap line on the menu is the one with the worst ratio of hands to money.

The one trial that cut the hour in half

There is remarkably little research on how long a massage should be, and essentially none on head spa specifically. What exists is a dosing trial published in the Annals of Family Medicine in 2014 by Karen Sherman and colleagues, which did the obvious experiment nobody else has: it held the technique constant and moved the clock. Two hundred and twenty-eight adults with chronic non-specific neck pain were randomised to four weeks of 30-minute massages twice or three times weekly, 60-minute massages once, twice or three times weekly, or a wait list.

The two outcomes were set in advance: a clinically meaningful gain on the Neck Disability Index, and a drop of at least 30 percent in pain. Sixty minutes twice a week beat the wait list on both, with a relative risk of 3.41 for function and 2.30 for pain. Three times a week did better again, 4.98 and 2.73. The 30-minute schedules produced no significant benefit on either measure, at either frequency. Same hands, same room, same technique · half the clock, and the effect went away.

Be clear about what that does and does not transfer. It is a therapeutic protocol for a pain complaint, repeated over a month, and your 60 minutes in An Thượng on a Tuesday is not a course of treatment. Nobody has run this trial on gội đầu dưỡng sinh and I would not pretend otherwise. What survives the translation is the narrower finding, which is about dose rather than disease: in the one study that bothered to vary session length, the half-hour was indistinguishable from nothing. That is a strange result to shrug at while buying a half-hour.

The cheapest quarter of an hour in the city

Look at what the step between tiers actually costs, rather than what the tiers cost. Herbal Spa charges 550K for 45 minutes and 650K for 60: the extra quarter-hour is 100K, which is 6.7K a minute against the 12.2K a minute you were already paying. The extension costs about half price. Len Spa's same step is 90K, or 6K a minute. YURURI's is 100K. Reborn's is 130K, from 250K to 380K, the steepest of the four and still under 9K a minute.

Past the hour the pattern holds and in places gets sharper. Panda Spa goes from 550K at 60 minutes to 700K at 90 · 150K for an extra half-hour, 5K a minute, the cheapest block of time in the whole table. Herbal Spa and YURURI both charge 250K for that same 30-minute step. Reborn's 60 to 80 is 120K, 6K a minute. At those five houses there is no step up the menu where the marginal minute costs more than the average minute of the ritual you were about to buy. Reborn is the exception in the other direction, because its short wash starts so low that every step up raises its per-minute price.

So the honest conclusion is slightly deflating for a price guide: after about 45 minutes, money stops being the thing to optimise. In city-wide terms the 45-minute band runs 250K to 550K across four houses and the 60-minute band 380K to 650K across six · the gap is a bowl of mì quảng. What should decide the booking from there is what the added minutes contain, and how long you can actually lie still.

Longer is not the same as more scalp

Here is the trap at the top of the menu, and it has nothing to do with being overcharged. Take the pick's eight published head spa tiers, since they are the most legible example in the city: 25 minutes at 120K for the basic wash, 45 at 250K, 60 at 380K, a 70-minute warm stone sequence at 450K, the 80-minute signature at 500K, a 75-minute CO₂ skin detox at 600K, 95 minutes at 750K and a 105-minute skin recovery ritual at 850K. The prices behave exactly as described above. The contents do not simply accumulate.

Reborn publishes the signature's sequence step by step: herbal foot soak and tea, facial cleansing and exfoliation, a quartz-stone massage, herbal steam and a mask, scalp exfoliation, the double herbal wash, a nourishing hair mask, neck, shoulder and hand massage, blow-dry, then fruit and tea. Count them · ten items inside eighty minutes, which averages eight minutes apiece. The sum is right and the ritual is lovely. But do the comparison that menus never invite you to do: a 60-minute booking that spends its whole hour on scalp, neck and shoulders can put more hands on your head than a 95-minute booking that also has to get through your face and your feet.

My view, and it is the one piece of advice here I would argue about: if you came for the scalp, buy 60 minutes of scalp rather than 95 minutes of spa afternoon. The long tiers are not padded · they are a different purchase, closer to a facial with a hair wash attached, and worth every đồng on a day when that is what you want. The way to get the hour spent where you want it is to say so while you are still vertical: tập trung vào da đầu nhé · please concentrate on the scalp. The sequence walkthrough sets out what each segment is, so you know what you are asking to be shortened.

The dry is inside your minutes, and it does not scale

Nobody in Da Nang sends a guest out with wet hair. It is partly courtesy and partly climate: walking into 85 percent humidity damp means your scalp stays wet for hours, which is how the itch starts, and in wet season the dry is half of what you are buying. Budget eight to twelve minutes of it for shoulder-length hair, two or three for a crop.

That time comes out of your booking, not out of the house's goodwill. The standard this guide holds houses to is that booked minutes are hands-on minutes rather than checkout minutes · and a blow-dry is hands-on. So the arithmetic is unavoidable: in a 25-minute wash, the dry can be a third of everything you paid for, while in an 80-minute ritual it is an eighth. The overhead is fixed; only the massage expands.

Which produces a corollary no menu will ever print. Short hair gets a better ratio at the same price. Someone with a number-two crop buying the 45-minute tier is getting close to 45 minutes of hands; someone with long thick hair buying the identical tier spends a fifth of it being towelled and dried. That is not an argument for refusing the dry, which in this climate is the opposite of a favour to yourself. It is an argument for long hair to buy the longer tier · the fixed cost is the same, so you want more minutes behind it.

The number to say at the counter

Default to 60. It is the band where the published sequences stop reading as a wash with extras and start reading as a massage with a wash inside it, the city range is broad and well evidenced at 380K to 650K across six houses, and the money that gets you there from 45 is the cheapest money on the menu. If you only ever book one head spa in Da Nang, book that one, and ask for the pressure you actually want rather than the pressure you are given.

The 25- and 30-minute wash still has exactly one correct use, and it is a good one: a shower you could not give yourself. Sea salt from My Khe, sunscreen, and a day's worth of helmet sweat come off a scalp far better under someone else's hands and a bowl of boiled bồ kết than they do in a hotel bathroom · 120K to 350K across the four houses selling that tier. Buy it as hygiene with a scalp massage attached. Do not buy it expecting the thing the hour does.

Above 80 minutes, plan rather than wander. Those tiers need a message ahead · the booking piece explains which houses answer where · and they need clock arithmetic, because a house that closes at 20:00, which is the pick's published hour, cannot start a 105-minute ritual after about 18:15. Three sentences cover the rest. Gói này bao nhiêu phút ạ? asks how many minutes a line on the menu actually is, which is the only number worth comparing. Cho mình gói 60 phút nhé books the hour. And if you are already horizontal with twenty minutes left and the idea of standing up seems unreasonable, mình muốn thêm 30 phút nữa được không? · usually possible when the next bed is free, and the one upsell in this city I would take every time. Frequently asked

How long should a head spa be?

Sixty minutes is the right default, and the one piece of evidence on session length supports it: in a 2014 dosing trial in the Annals of Family Medicine, 228 adults with chronic neck pain were randomised to 30- or 60-minute massages, and only the 60-minute schedules beat the wait list on pain and function. Da Nang prices that tier at 380K to 650K across the six houses that publish menus. Go to 80 or 90 minutes when you want facial care, steam or stone work layered in rather than more scalp time.

Is a 30-minute head spa worth it in Da Nang?

Worth it as a wash, not as a treatment. A 25- to 30-minute line costs 120K to 350K at the four houses selling one, and it is the best way in the city to get sea salt, sunscreen and helmet sweat off your scalp. But it is also the worst value per minute at most houses, because the fixed parts of the visit · turning over the bed, the towels, the blow-dry, the tea · cost the same as they do in a 90-minute ritual. In five of the six published menus, the longest ritual costs less per minute than the shortest.

Is a 90-minute head spa better than a 60-minute one?

It is longer and usually cheaper per minute, but it is not automatically more scalp work. Long tiers earn their extra time by adding segments: herbal steam, hot or quartz stones, facial cleansing, a foot soak, skin treatments. The guide's pick publishes an 80-minute signature with ten steps in it, which averages eight minutes each. If what you want is hands on your head, an hour spent entirely on scalp, neck and shoulders delivers more of it than 95 minutes spread across your face and feet.

How much does a one-hour head spa cost in Da Nang?

380K to 650K VND, roughly 15 to 26 US dollars, across the six Da Nang houses that publish a full price list as of 2 October 2026. Per minute that is about 6.3K at the cheapest and 10.8K at the dearest. Stepping up from the 45-minute tier to the hour costs only 90K to 130K at the houses selling both, which makes it the least expensive quarter-hour on any of those menus.
