Neck safety · 8 min · 6 October 2026

The neck in the basin: what the stroke papers say, and what Da Nang's furniture changes

Whether a head spa is safe for your neck · the 54 cases in the entire published literature, why a 190 cm Vietnamese washing bed removes the mechanism, the one movement to decline at the end of the shoulder work, and the symptoms that arrive three days late.

In short
  • The worry has a name and a startlingly short record. A 2025 scoping review in the American Journal of Emergency Medicine pooled 22 studies and found 54 published cases of beauty parlor stroke syndrome in total · 42 of them in salons, 8 in dental chairs. One Swiss stroke registry logged ten hairdresser-related events across eleven years.
  • The mechanism in those papers is a seated body with the head tipped back over a hard rim. Da Nang does not usually work that way: the giường gội đầu is a washing bed about 190 cm long, so your torso is horizontal and your nape rests in a padded notch. That is different geometry, not just a softer lounger.
  • The movement worth declining is not the wash. It is the fast rotational neck crack a minority of technicians add to the end of the shoulder segment · không vặn cổ nhé · and, if you have known arterial disease, the hot standing blow-dry.
  • None of this argues for buying less. The safe booking is the ordinary one: an hour, flat on the bed, 380K to 650K on the price table. The posture the case reports describe is the ten-minute seated rinse over a barber's sink.

A syndrome with 54 cases in it

The term was coined in 1993, in JAMA, by a neurologist in New York called Michael Weintraub, who had treated five women admitted with neurological symptoms after having their hair shampooed at a salon. He named it beauty parlor stroke syndrome and the phrase has outlived almost everything written about it, which is a problem, because what has been written about it is very thin.

The most recent attempt to count is a scoping review published in the American Journal of Emergency Medicine in 2025. The authors searched three databases for anything with original data linking neck hyperextension to a neurological deficit, pulled 22 studies, and arrived at 54 cases. Fifty-four, in the global published record, at all ages between 13 and 85, roughly four in five of them women. Forty-two happened at a hair salon; eight happened in a dentist's chair, which tells you the hairdryer was never the point. The usual mechanisms were mechanical pinching of a vertebral artery and tearing of a cervical artery wall.

The other number worth having comes from Lausanne, where a stroke registry was read forward from 2002 to 2013 looking specifically for events tied to a hairdresser visit. Eleven years produced ten · nine strokes and one transient attack. The causes were all over the map: two carotid dissections, two cardiac, two small-vessel, two other, two never established. The authors were honest enough to coin a term for the ones that were probably coincidence, and honest enough to finish on a sentence most health writing would have buried: available data are insufficient for specific preventive recommendations. Our reading is that a risk this small does not deserve a cancelled booking. It deserves about one sentence of attention, aimed at the right thing.

The furniture is the whole argument

Picture the posture in those case reports, because it is specific. You are sitting upright in a salon chair. The chair rolls back toward a ceramic sink and your head goes over the rim, held there for five or ten minutes while somebody works above you. Your shoulders are still at seated height, so the cervical spine has to bridge the gap on its own, and the rim is the fulcrum it bridges across. That is the geometry that puts a vertebral artery under stretch where it rounds the top vertebra.

Da Nang mostly sells a different object. The standard item in Vietnamese spa-furniture catalogues is the giường gội đầu · literally the hair-washing bed · listed at roughly 190 cm long, 70 cm wide and 70 cm high, with custom builds out to 220 cm for houses expecting larger guests, one-piece foam under faux leather, and a porcelain or moulded-plastic basin at the head end. They run about 2 to 7 million đồng depending on the basin and whether the frame also does massage or a foot soak. You lie on it full length, shoes on, and your nape settles into a notch with the rest of you in line behind it.

Your neck is still cradled over a basin, which is how the sequence walkthrough describes it and the description is right. But it is cradled from a lying body rather than a sitting one, and the extension angle is a fraction of the salon version. Here is the part I find genuinely funny: nobody in this city sells the bed on that basis. The bed exists because you cannot keep a person in a salon chair for sixty minutes and because gội đầu dưỡng sinh is a massage that happens to involve shampoo. The safer neck is a side effect of selling minutes. It is the most underrated thing about the Vietnamese version of this ritual and it appears on no menu anywhere.

Where the old posture still turns up here

Not every wash in Da Nang happens on a bed, and the exceptions are predictable. Barbershops that throw a rinse in with a cut often use a chair-and-sink, because the wash is a ten-minute courtesy rather than the product. The same goes for some neighbourhood counters selling a quick gội đầu at street prices, and for a scattering of hotel salons and clinic-style rooms fitted out with Western salon furniture · which is worth knowing if you are deciding between the Vietnamese houses and the Korean-style ones, a choice laid out here. None of those are dangerous. They are simply the posture the literature is about, held for a tenth as long.

The genuine Da Nang variable is time rather than angle. An hour is a long stretch in any single position, and a good position held for sixty minutes still asks something of the neck. If yours is already unhappy · a day on a rented Vision, a long-haul seat, a hotel pillow the shape of a brick · say so and ask for padding: cho mình cái khăn kê cổ gets a rolled towel under the nape and takes a few degrees out of the angle. It costs nothing and it is the single most useful request in this whole article.

So look at the room before you pay, not after. If there are beds, you are lying down. If there are salon chairs, you will be tipping back, and for a long ritual that is worth one question at the counter about whether a bed is free. The neighbourhood guide is the faster way to find the house types clustered near wherever you are staying.

The crack at the end of the shoulder work

This is the paragraph to actually act on. Most Da Nang rituals close the neck and shoulder segment with a stretch of some kind, and a minority of technicians finish that stretch with a quick rotational thrust that produces a pop. It feels, for about four seconds, tremendous. It is also the one movement in the whole hour that Vietnamese doctors keep warning about in their own domestic press, which has run repeated pieces on the vogue for neck and spine adjustment.

Tâm Anh General Hospital published one of them, quoting the head of neurology at its neuroscience centre, Dr Nguyễn Thị Minh Đức, on the mechanism: a sudden, large-amplitude rotation of the neck can tear the inner lining of an artery, and the resulting dissection either narrows the vessel or throws a clot downstream. The figure she cites from the American Stroke Association is the one that reframes it · dissection accounts for only around 2 percent of strokes overall, but something closer to a quarter of strokes in younger adults. The risk is higher with high blood pressure, with osteoporosis, with a clotting disorder or anticoagulant medication, and with any neck injury in the recent past. Her recommendation is that manipulation belongs to trained, licensed practitioners after an actual examination.

My position is blunter than the hospital's, because the economics here are not ambiguous. Decline the crack whatever your risk profile. A head spa technician in Da Nang is trained in massage and is often very good at it; nothing in a 380K to 650K hour buys a cervical examination, and the pop is theatre rather than treatment. Không vặn cổ nhé · please don't twist my neck · settles it at the counter. If you want the stretch without the thrust, xoa bóp thôi, không bẻ nhé asks for massage only. And do not let any of this talk you out of the segment itself: the slow kneading along the upper trapezius is the best twenty minutes of the booking, and on an arrival day it is most of the reason to go at all.

The blow-dry is the part the registry blamed

Back to Lausanne for the detail nobody expects. Of those ten events, the two patients who had existing narrowing in the arteries inside the skull were thought to have had their strokes not from the basin but from acute circulatory change during hot-air hair drying · a drop in blood pressure in somebody whose brain was already living on thin margins. The hairdryer, in other words, was the better suspect than the sink.

Da Nang amplifies exactly that moment. Houses here dry hot and fast, because sending a guest into 85 percent humidity with damp hair is a discourtesy and because in wet season the dry is the product. You have been horizontal and warm for an hour, often with a steam canopy or a hot towel in the middle of it. Then you stand up, and the room is air-conditioned to 24 degrees while the street outside is 33. Standing up fast from warm and reclined is a textbook recipe for a grey-out in anyone, and a genuine hazard in a narrow group of people.

The fix is embarrassingly mundane. Be sat up for the dry rather than stood up for it · most houses do this by default, with the lounger raised and a mirror in front of you. Drink the tea they bring; it is there for this. Sấy mát nhé asks for cooler air if the heat on your scalp is too much. And give yourself a minute at the door before you walk into the afternoon instead of going straight from the chair to a Grab.

The three-day lag, and the sentence to say if it applies to you

The symptom list in the scoping review is dizziness, disturbed balance and headache, more often than anything dramatic. That ordering matters, because those are back-of-the-brain symptoms and the public stroke test is not built for the back of the brain: face droop, arm weakness, slurred speech describe the front. Someone whose vertebral artery is the problem can pass that check while something is quite clearly wrong.

The lag is the dangerous part. In a case published in 2023, a healthy 23-year-old man felt a pain in the nape of his neck while it was held extended for a barber's shave, went home, and did not develop vertigo, nausea and vomiting until three or four days later; the scan then showed an infarct in his cerebellum. So the rule is about sequence rather than timing. New, sharp neck pain during or just after the hour, followed in the next few days by vertigo, double vision, unsteadiness on your feet, slurred speech or numbness down one side, is an emergency department rather than a wait-and-see. Da Nang has run 115 as its single pre-hospital emergency number citywide since October 2025, and the main public hospital, Bệnh viện Đà Nẵng, is at 124 Hải Phòng in Hải Châu, which is ten minutes from most of the beach-side spa cluster.

There is a short list of people who should say something at the counter rather than reading this and shrugging: anyone with known carotid or vertebral artery disease, anyone who has had a dissection before, anyone on anticoagulants, anyone with a connective-tissue disorder, and anyone whose neck was injured in the last few weeks. The whole conversation is two requests · no neck twisting, and keep me as flat as you can · and a counter in An Thượng will hear both without blinking. Everybody else: book the hour, lie down, take the ear work or turn it down in four words, and spend your one sentence of vigilance on the stretch at the end rather than on the basin at the start.

Frequently asked

Is a head spa safe for your neck?

For almost everyone, yes · and the Vietnamese version is better designed for it than a Western salon wash. Da Nang houses work from a washing bed around 190 cm long, so you lie flat with your nape in a padded notch rather than sitting upright with your head tipped back over a sink rim, which is the posture the case reports describe. The two things worth managing are duration · ask for a rolled towel under the nape for a 60-minute ritual · and declining any fast neck twist at the end of the shoulder massage.

Can you have a stroke from getting your hair washed?

It is documented and it is very rare. A 2025 scoping review in the American Journal of Emergency Medicine pooled 22 studies and found 54 published cases in total, 42 of them at a hair salon, across all ages from 13 to 85. A Swiss stroke registry read across eleven years turned up ten hairdresser-related events, and its authors concluded some of those were probably coincidence and that the data do not support specific preventive advice. Risk concentrates in people with existing arterial disease.

Should I let a massage therapist crack my neck in Vietnam?

No, and this is the one firm recommendation on the page. Vietnamese hospitals have published warnings about the neck-adjustment trend: a sudden large-amplitude rotation can tear the inner lining of an artery, and that dissection causes around a quarter of strokes in younger adults even though it causes only about 2 percent overall. A head spa technician is trained in massage, not cervical manipulation. The phrase at the counter is không vặn cổ nhé · please don't twist my neck · and the kneading, which is the good part, stays in.

What is beauty parlor stroke syndrome?

A term coined by the neurologist Michael Weintraub in JAMA in 1993, after he treated five women admitted with neurological symptoms following a salon shampoo. It describes a stroke or transient attack caused by a vertebral artery being pinched or a cervical artery being torn when the neck is rotated or bent backwards for a sustained period · over a shampoo sink, in a dental chair, during a shave. Reported cases run to a few dozen worldwide.
