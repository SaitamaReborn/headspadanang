Coloured hair · 7 min · 17 September 2026

Dyed, bleached, freshly toned: what a Da Nang herbal wash does to your colour

Whether a Vietnamese head spa fades colour-treated hair · the 48-to-72-hour rule, why saponin is not sulfate, the two steps on the menu that genuinely lift pigment, and the four sentences to say at the counter.

In short
  • Timing settles it, not the herbs. Head spa operators who handle chemically processed hair put the gap at 48 to 72 hours between a colour, bleach or keratin service and a head spa. Past that window, a herbal wash is unremarkable for dyed hair.
  • A real bồ kết decoction cleans with saponin, not sulfate · the thing colour-safe shampoos advertise on the bottle is what the locust pod does by default. Bottled herbal formulas are a different question.
  • Two steps lift pigment and neither is the shampoo: basin water run hot, and the tẩy da đầu scalp scrub layered into the tiers from about 380K upward. Both are declinable in one sentence.
  • With colour under a week old, the 120K entry wash on the tier table is the safer purchase than the 500K signature. It is the one case on this site where buying fewer minutes buys a better outcome.

The gap that decides everything

The question arrives in the same shape every time. Someone has a balayage they paid a lot for at home, or a toner laid down at a Hải Châu salon on Tuesday, and a 60-minute ritual booked for Thursday. The answer has almost nothing to do with what is in the bowl and almost everything to do with the calendar.

Colour does not finish when you leave the chair. The cuticle spends the next day or two settling back down over pigment that was chemically pushed underneath it, and anything that prises it open again in that window · heat, water, friction, an alkaline product · takes some of that pigment back out with it. Head spas that deal with chemically processed hair publish the same number. Chandee, a head spa in Bangkok's Silom district, sets the minimum gap at 48 to 72 hours and names clarifying and detox formulas as the specific hazard rather than the wash itself. Colourists give the same window for the first shampoo at home, for the same reason.

The practical version for a Da Nang week: if you coloured before you flew, the window was served in the air and you can stop worrying. If you are colouring here · and plenty of visitors do, because a full colour in this city costs less than a trim in most of the places they flew from · then the ordering matters. Head spa first, colour second. Or colour Monday, head spa Thursday. Both on the same afternoon is the one genuinely bad plan, and it is the plan the salons that sell both services will cheerfully let you book.

Saponin is not sulfate

The mechanism is worth a paragraph, because most people have the anxiety pointed in the wrong direction. Commercial shampoo cleans with sulfates · sodium lauryl or laureth sulfate · which are efficient, cheap and indiscriminate, and which are the reason every colour-care line on the shelf sells itself on the word sulfate-free. A bồ kết decoction cleans with saponin, the plant compound in roasted locust pods that raises that thin, disappointing first lather. It is a weaker surfactant and it was never anything else.

So the pot on the burner is, on this one axis, kinder to your colour than the bottle in your hotel bathroom. People assume the reverse: that something boiled from bark and pods must be harsher than a pump bottle with French on the label. It is not. The catch is the one covered in what is actually in the bowl · a good number of Da Nang houses work from a bottled bồ kết-and-pomelo formula rather than a fresh decoction, and a bottled formula contains whatever the manufacturer put in it, sulfates included. Nobody at the 120K counter is going to produce an ingredients list. If your colour is new enough to be worth protecting, the amber, slightly cloudy jug is the thing to ask for by name: gội bồ kết.

The two steps that actually lift pigment

Neither of them is the shampoo. The first is water temperature. Basin water in Da Nang runs warm to frankly hot, partly because heat against the scalp is a large share of why the first ten minutes feel the way they do, and partly because in twenty years nobody has asked for anything else. Heat opens the cuticle; an open cuticle sheds pigment into the drain. This is the least controversial claim in all of colour care and the easiest thing on the list to fix, because it costs one sentence and changes nothing else about the ritual. Warm rather than hot, and a cool rinse at the end if the technician offers one.

The second is tẩy da đầu, the scalp exfoliation, printed on English menus as a scrub, a peel, a purifying step or a detox. It appears in the tiers from roughly 380K upward and it is a clarifying treatment by design · its entire job is to strip sebum and product residue off the skin. On the scalp that is exactly what you want. On three-week-old colour it is a non-event. On a toner applied forty-eight hours ago it is the most aggressive item on the menu, and a technician's fingers do not stop politely at the hairline.

There is a third, smaller one at the far end: the blow-dry. Da Nang houses dry hot and fast, because sending someone out into eighty-five percent humidity with damp hair is a discourtesy, and because a proper finish is the last thing the room is judged on. Hot air is heat on an already-warmed cuticle. Asking for cooler air costs you three or four minutes of sitting there and nothing else.

Book down, not up

Everywhere else on this guide the advice runs in one direction. Minutes are the product, the entry wash is a wash, the herbs need dwell time, buy the longer tier. With colour less than a week old, invert all of it.

The 25-minute entry wash at around 120K is the gentlest item available in this city. One shampoo pass, warm water, a rinse, a dry, out. No steam, no exfoliation, no forty minutes of sustained friction along the lengths. The 80-minute signature at 500K · the correct booking for almost everybody else · stacks a double shampoo, a herbal steam and usually a scalp scrub on top of one another, and a steam cabinet is a heat treatment whatever else the menu calls it. The 70-minute warm-stone ritual at about 450K sits in between and is the one we would actually pick at day five or six, because the mask-and-warm-towel segment is closer to a repair than to an attack. Tier-by-tier numbers are on the prices page.

We would not give this advice to the same person a fortnight later. At that point the colour has locked, the pigment that was going to leave has left, and the long ritual is the better hour by a distance. The downgrade is a two-week measure, not a policy.

What Da Nang actually does to your colour

The head spa takes the blame for a week that was not its fault. Consider what else happens to a head of dyed hair on a Da Nang itinerary: four mornings in the sea at Mỹ Khê, where salt water draws moisture out of the strand and leaves blonde and ash tones reaching for brass. An afternoon in a hotel pool, where chlorine does something considerably less subtle. And September sun at 16 degrees north, which is still strong enough at midday to be doing quiet oxidative work on the top two inches whether or not it feels hot under cloud.

Then, on day five, a head spa · the one appointment where you sit still for an hour and actually look at your hair in a mirror afterwards. Of course that is where the fade becomes visible. If you are staying in An Thượng or Mỹ An, the walk from the sand to a head spa door is about four minutes, which is precisely the problem: the two things happen close enough together to look causal. Our reading is that for anyone who has been swimming daily, the conditioning mask and its ten minutes under a warm towel are the most restorative thing they will do to their hair all week. Rinse the salt out at the beach shower before you go, and the wash has less to fight. Neighbourhood-by-neighbourhood options are mapped on where to go.

Four sentences at the counter

Say these before you recline, not once your neck is already in the cradle. At the counter they are logistics; on the lounger they read as a complaint about work in progress.

Tóc tôi mới nhuộm · my hair is freshly dyed. This one alone usually produces the right behaviour without anything further, because a technician who hears it adjusts the water without being asked. Nước ấm thôi nhé · warm water only. Không tẩy da đầu nhé · no scalp scrub. Sấy mát nhé · cool blow-dry. If your hair is bleached rather than dyed, the word is tóc tẩy, and it is worth using, because bleached hair is porous in a way that changes how fast everything in the basin moves in and out of it.

None of this is a special request in any awkward sense. Water temperature and product choice are exactly the two things a technician expects to calibrate, and the rest of the sequence · what happens, in what order, for how long · runs the way it always does. Frequently asked

Is a head spa safe for color-treated hair?

Yes, once you are past 48 to 72 hours from the colour service. Head spas that work with chemically processed hair set that as the minimum gap, and flag clarifying or detox scrubs rather than the wash itself as the risk. Outside that window, a herbal wash is one of the gentler things you can do to dyed hair.

How long after dyeing my hair should I wait before a head spa?

48 to 72 hours is the figure published by head spa operators and it matches what colourists say about the first shampoo at home. In Da Nang the practical rule is to book the head spa before the salon appointment, or leave two clear days between them · never both on the same afternoon.

Will Vietnamese herbal shampoo strip my hair colour?

A genuine bồ kết decoction cleans with plant saponins rather than sulfates, which makes it milder on colour than most commercial shampoo. The heat of the basin water and the tẩy da đầu scalp exfoliation lift far more pigment than the shampoo does, and both can be declined at the counter.

Can I get a head spa with bleached hair or a keratin treatment?

Yes, after the waiting period your stylist gave you · usually 72 hours for keratin. Sulfate-free washing is the standard aftercare instruction for keratin, and a locust-pod decoction is sulfate-free by nature, so it suits the treatment better than most hotel shampoo. Say tóc tẩy for bleached hair and skip the scalp scrub.
